August Offensive: A Preview of China's LLM Releases for August 2026

August Offensive: A Preview of China's LLM Releases for August 2026

Chinese model vendors spent the end of July "handing in their homework" en masse, and August's calendar is already full: multiple signals point to the official DeepSeek V4-Pro release on August 3, while brokerage notes flag Zhipu GLM-5.3 and MiniMax M3Pro as major catalysts. With the parameter race back in the 2-trillion era and Chinese models holding all top five spots by call volume on OpenRouter, every launch event this August is worth watching.

Introduction: A Battlefield With No Off-Season

For Chinese foundation-model vendors, the 2026 calendar has no slow season. July 31 alone delivered a three-in-a-row — MiniMax H3, ByteDance Seedance 2.5, and the official DeepSeek V4-Flash release all arrived on the same day; a week earlier, on July 27, Moonshot AI open-sourced Kimi K3, the largest model in the world by parameter count. And judging by multiple brokerage research reports and industry signals, August's release slate is just as dense: the official DeepSeek V4-Pro, Zhipu GLM-5.3, and MiniMax M3Pro are all in the final countdown. Drawing on the latest developments from late July and on market signals, this post rounds up the Chinese models most worth anticipating in August — and the industry trends behind them.

What Makes the Official DeepSeek V4-Pro Release Worth Watching?

If there is one "must-see launch" in August, it is DeepSeek's. Timing signals point to August 3. When the company opened the V4-Flash official API beta on July 31, it explicitly teased that V4-Pro would launch officially in early August. Several external signals further corroborate that schedule:
  • SiliconFlow sharply raised the cache-hit price for V4 Pro on August 3: from ¥0.1 to ¥1.0 per million tokens. The tenfold jump is widely read across the industry as "price positioning" ahead of the official launch;
  • Developer communities noticed that some DeepSeek APIs can now correctly answer questions they previously got wrong — a staged rollout is ramping up in waves;
  • The V4 official release had originally been slated for mid-July and slipped once; now the word is that "the wait won't be long this time."
Performance expectations: The prevailing view is that V4's official release will land somewhere between GLM-5.2 and Claude Opus 4.8. For reference, V4-Flash (284B total parameters / 13B active), which entered public beta on July 31, already brings a substantial upgrade in agentic and coding capability: a score of 82.7 on Terminal Bench 2.1, native support for the Responses API, Codex compatibility, and the lowest pricing of any Chinese frontier model. The bonus: DeepSeek's official AI coding tool Harness has opened internal testing and is widely expected to debut alongside the official V4-Pro release. Add a recently closed Series A of roughly ¥51 billion (at a valuation near ¥400 billion), and V4-Pro's release cadence and commercialization playbook deserve close attention.

2. The Next Baton in Brokerage Research: GLM-5.3 and MiniMax M3Pro

The market has pinned August's other two high-stakes variables on Zhipu and MiniMax.
  • Zhipu GLM-5.3: In its weekly report, Zheshang Securities explicitly identifies model version iterations (Zhipu GLM-5.3, MiniMax M3Pro) as important sector catalysts going forward. Zhipu shipped GLM-5.2 only in June (with 1M lossless context), and its coding model has been rated SOTA on multiple leaderboards, so GLM-5.3 will most likely keep betting on long context plus coding and agent capability.
  • MiniMax M3Pro: MiniMax released H3, its first open-source omni-modal generation model, on July 31, while the next flagship, M3Pro, is reported to carry more than 2 trillion parameters. MiniMax is also expected to qualify for and formally join the Southbound Stock Connect in August — capital-market catalysts moving in lockstep with product launches, an intent that is hard to miss.
Kaiyuan Securities likewise notes in its research that "the official V4-Pro release will ship as soon as possible," putting DeepSeek's cadence alongside the other two as a core watch item.

August's anticipation rests on the dense cluster of releases at the end of July. Looking back at that wave:

4. The Bigger Picture: The Parameter Race Restarts, 2 Trillion Becomes Table Stakes

Put those names side by side and one signal is unmistakable — the parameter race is officially back. According to the Economic Observer, China's leading model vendors are collectively pushing past 2 trillion parameters: Kimi K3 (2.78T), Qwen3.8 Max (2.4T), ERNIE 5.0 (2.4T), and MiniMax's next generation (2T+) — 2 trillion is now the entry ticket, and 3 trillion is seen as the next stop. The technical focus has shifted in step: long-horizon tasks, coding, agentic behavior, and multimodality have become the four directions. V4-Flash's reinforcement of agent capability, GLM's coding SOTA, and H3's omni-modal unification are all concrete footnotes to that shift.

5. Leading Globally: All Top Five on OpenRouter Are Chinese Models

By August 2026, the question "can Chinese models compete?" had turned into a matter of data:
  • Chinese companies hold all five top spots on OpenRouter's call-volume leaderboard: Xiaomi MiMo-V2.5 tops it with 10.5 trillion Tokens in a single week (first on both the weekly and monthly charts, more than a sixfold increase in two months), two DeepSeek models sit at second and fifth, and Tencent Hunyuan Hy3 ranks third with a week-over-week gain above 999%;
  • Hugging Face downloads: Chinese open-source models rank first globally in cumulative downloads, with Alibaba's Qwen series topping the chart at over 1 billion downloads;
  • One frequently cited figure: 80% of US AI startups use Chinese open-source models in their fundraising pitches, and more than 200 derivative models built on Chinese foundation models appear every day.
Every launch in August will rewrite that leaderboard directly.

6. Price Cuts and Open Source: Moving AI From "Usable" to "Easy to Use"

Running parallel to the parameter race is another thread — price cuts plus open source:
  • V4-Flash enters at the lowest price point among Chinese frontier models; SiliconFlow's steep hike to V4 Pro's cache-hit price actually suggests that the low price is a strategic move to subsidize its way to market share;
  • Kimi K3's full weights were released on July 27; MiniMax H3 became the first open-source mainstream multimodal generation model and plans to open its weights for enterprise on-premises deployment;
  • Institutional views hold that price cuts and open source are pushing AI from "usable" toward "easy to use," and that enterprise AI deployment could accelerate markedly — competition among foundation models is spreading from "who is stronger" to "who is cheaper and easier to use."

Can Domestic Chip Supply Keep Up?

Behind the dense release schedule is a domestic relay on compute supply. A China Merchants Securities International research note points out that mass production of domestic chips in the second half — such as Huawei's Ascend 950 series — will be a significant catalyst for model efficiency, with inference costs expected to keep falling. Infrastructure is filling in at the same pace: on August 2, the National Supercomputing Internet officially launched API access and model files for DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731; the platform has become the country's first hundred-thousand-card-class supercomputing-and-intelligence resource pool, and the AI community has aggregated more than 1,700 open-source models from China and abroad. Compute, models, and platforms accelerating together are what give August's packed launch calendar its confidence.

Conclusion: August Is Bound to Be Eventful

Back to the schedule from the opening: August 3 may be the day the official DeepSeek V4-Pro lands; after that, GLM-5.3 and MiniMax M3Pro are poised to go; add domestic chip mass production as a catalyst and leaderboard data that keeps refreshing — August's Chinese LLM battlefield will have launches, price hikes, open-source drops, and funding rounds, but no off-season. What to ship, how big, and how cheap — those are the three questions every launch event this month has to answer.
