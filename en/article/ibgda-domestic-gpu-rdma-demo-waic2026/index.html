Why Is Domestic Compute "Strong at the Single Point, Weak as a System"?

Why does this forum matter so much? Answering that means first seeing the structural imbalance domestic compute faces today. Qiyi Moore's product portfolio spans three scales:
Interconnect scale Scope Corresponding products
Scale-Out Inter-node networking RDMA NICs · ToR/Spine switching
Scale-Up XPU-to-XPU interconnect within a supernode OSU IO chiplets · CPO optical engines

The core predicament is "strong at the single point, weak as a system": on-die, die-to-die, node-to-node, and cluster-to-cluster interconnect each grew up as its own silo, with no unified full-stack view and no system-level coordination — so system-level performance never scales linearly with the number of chips. How Do Domestic GPUs Work Together with RDMA NICs?

3.1 Background: Why Does MoE Need IBGDA?

In typical MoE (Mixture of Experts) training or inference, Expert Parallelism communication has two core phases:
  • Dispatch: route each token to its corresponding experts
  • Combine: aggregate the expert outputs back onto the original tokens
Both phases involve enormous volumes of fine-grained data exchange — the perennial headache of AI cluster networking. In conventional cross-node communication, the GPU has to relay its instructions through the CPU, which adds extra latency and CPU overhead. IBGDA (InfiniBand GPU Direct Async) exists precisely to solve this: by bypassing the CPU on the data path, it sharply cuts the wait for instruction relay and delivers higher throughput and lower latency in All-to-All traffic.

3.2 The International Benchmark and the Domestic Gap

Camp Status quo
🟢 NVIDIA Deep GPU + ConnectX NIC co-design made it the first to deliver an IBGDA implementation
🟡 Domestic GPUs Must support NVSHMEM-like programming, the latest NCCL communication libraries, DeepEP and other collective communication libraries
🟡 Domestic RDMA NICs Must be adapted to domestic GPUs at both the software and hardware levels before GPUs can talk to NICs efficiently
🔴 Deployment at scale Large-scale deployments of domestic GPUs running over domestic RDMA NICs remain relatively rare
Qiyi Moore's IBGDA Demo exists to fill exactly that gap.

4.2 The Optical Interconnect Roadmap: The Closer, the Stronger

The whitepaper lays out a clear five-stage path for optics moving from "pluggable" to "intrinsic":
The five-stage evolution of optical interconnect: pluggable optical modules → NPO → CPO → OIO → intrinsic optics (integrated inside the compute die). The closer the optics sit to the compute unit, the shorter the reach and the more the DSP can be dropped, which lowers latency, simplifies system design, and cuts power and cost. Qiyi Moore is focused on the CPO stage and is pushing toward the next stage together with ecosystem partners.

4.3 Qiyi Moore's Chiplet Layout

Qiyi Moore has already laid out, well ahead of time, the convergence path between its OSU (Optical Scale-Up) IO chiplet products and future CPO iterations.
The OSU IO chiplet + CPO converged architecture above: on an advanced packaging substrate, the xPU chiplet (domestic GPU/NPU), the OSU IO chiplet (electrical-to-optical modulation), and the CPO co-packaged optical module (laser / modulator / optical waveguide / photodetector) work in concert and transmit outward over fiber. Compared with conventional pluggable approaches, it delivers lower latency, lower power, and higher bandwidth density.

5. Winning Together: The "Domestic Supernode Ecosystem Co-Building Initiative" Officially Launches

5.1 Industry Insight: Three Exponential Curves Pulling at Once

Demand for large-model compute is being driven by three exponential curves at the same time:
  1. Scaling laws on the training side — model scale and data scale expanding in lockstep
  2. Test-time scaling on the inference side — compute per inference growing exponentially
  3. An explosion in call volume driven by agent applications — the agent paradigm sending inference call frequency sharply higher
Which means what actually decides the landscape increasingly comes down to interconnect, full systems, software, and system-level coordination.
Yang Lian, secretary-general of the Shanghai Computing Network Association, noted that domestic supernodes still face fragmentation and chokepoint risk in system architecture definition, Scale-Up interconnect protocols, hardware-software co-optimization, and cluster RAS capabilities.
