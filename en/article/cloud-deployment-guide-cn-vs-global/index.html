Cloud Deployment in China and Abroad: Domestic Cloud, Overseas VPS, and the Cloudflare Free Stack

In 2026, there have never been more ways to put a project online: the three big domestic cloud providers are locked in a price war, capable-but-cheap overseas VPS plans are everywhere, and Cloudflare has pushed "free" about as far as it will go. This article lines up the three paths — domestic cloud, overseas cloud, and edge platforms — side by side, so you can find the deployment strategy that actually fits your situation.

Why Is Choosing Where to Deploy Getting Harder?

A few years ago, "going to the cloud" basically meant buying a cloud server (IaaS): pick one of Alibaba Cloud, Tencent Cloud, or AWS and you were done. Today, deployment has split into three fundamentally different paths:
Path Examples Form Key traits
Domestic cloud servers Alibaba Cloud / Tencent Cloud / Huawei Cloud IaaS virtual machines Fast access inside China, requires an ICP filing, expensive bandwidth
Overseas cloud AWS/Azure/GCP + Vultr/Hetzner IaaS/PaaS No filing required, global regions, billed in USD, unstable latency from China
Edge platforms Cloudflare Pages/Workers Static hosting + serverless Astonishing free tiers, global CDN, near-zero ops
The real question isn't "which provider is best" but which path matches the shape of your business. Let's take them one by one.

Is Domestic Cloud Actually Cheap Now?

According to IDC, China's public cloud IaaS market topped 400 billion yuan in 2025, with Alibaba Cloud, Tencent Cloud, and Huawei Cloud holding the top three spots. In 2026 the competition has turned into an outright price war: entry-level specs have been pushed down to a few dozen yuan a year.

Spec Alibaba Cloud Tencent Cloud Huawei Cloud

A few "unwritten rules" worth paying attention to when choosing a plan:
  1. Signup price isn't the long-term price: many of these deals are new-customer-only, and renewal doubles the bill. Favor promotions that explicitly state "same price on renewal" (like Alibaba Cloud's 99 Plan or Tencent Cloud's plans that treat new and existing customers alike).
  2. Bandwidth is a hidden expense: cheap tiers often ship with 1-3 Mbps of fixed bandwidth or a "peak bandwidth" figure, so be careful with image-heavy sites and download mirrors. By comparison, Alibaba Cloud's lightweight 200 Mbps peak with unmetered traffic is the friendliest option for sudden spikes.
  3. An ICP filing is mandatory: any mainland China node requires one, and the process takes 7-20 business days. If you can't wait, pick a Hong Kong or overseas node — no filing, but noticeably slower access from mainland China.

Where Domestic Cloud Genuinely Wins

  • Network quality: multi-line BGP access means low, stable latency in every province — something an overseas VPS simply cannot match.
  • Mature ecosystem: object storage, CDN, SMS, payments, and AI services integrated in one place, so you get to production fast.
  • Payments and compliance: pay directly with WeChat Pay or Alipay, with invoicing, real-name verification, and regulatory oversight handled end to end — a good fit for legitimate commercial use.
China Mobile Cloud, JD Cloud, and Baidu AI Cloud are also buying market share with aggressive pricing (China Mobile Cloud 2 vCPU / 4 GB at roughly 151 yuan a year, Huawei Cloud 2 vCPU / 2 GB at 38 yuan a year). They're worth comparing when budget is tight, though the big three still lead on ecosystem breadth and documentation.

2. Overseas Cloud: Freedom Without a Filing, Priced in Dollars

Overseas deployment comes in two flavors: hyperscale public cloud (AWS, Azure, GCP) and developer-friendly VPS (Vultr, DigitalOcean, Hetzner, Linode).

Hyperscalers vs. VPS: Completely Different Positioning

Dimension AWS / Azure / GCP Vultr / DO / Hetzner
Positioning Full-stack enterprise cloud platform Lightweight, developer-friendly IaaS
Entry price Usage-based; even the smallest instance runs $5-10/month, with egress billed separately From $2.5-6/month
Free credits A free tier for the first 12 months Deposit-matched credit (Vultr $300 / DO $200)
Complexity High; bills spiral easily (egress is the assassin) Low; hourly billing, transparent pricing
Access from China Standard international routes, visible packet loss at peak hours Same; neither offers CN2 GIA optimized routing
Key numbers (2026 comparison): for the same 2 vCPU / 4 GB spec, a Hetzner CX22 costs about €4.5/month (including 20 TB of egress), DigitalOcean/Vultr about $24/month (including 1-3 TB), while AWS EC2 costs several times more with egress billed on top — which is exactly where "the same job can cost 10-50× more on AWS" comes from.

Each Provider's Strength, at a Glance

  • Hetzner (Germany): the price-performance king of Europe, with 20 TB of free egress that is effectively unlimited — the default choice for EU workloads and GDPR compliance.
  • Vultr: 32 data centers worldwide, covering Tokyo, Seoul, Singapore, and other Asian locations, the most flexible hourly billing, plus GPU instances.
  • DigitalOcean: the industry benchmark for docs and tutorials; App Platform takes you from git push to live, and Kubernetes and managed databases work out of the box.
  • AWS/Azure/GCP: the ceiling on ecosystem, suited to multinational operations and larger projects that need managed pieces like S3 or a cloud database.
The pain points of overseas VPS are equally clear: latency from mainland China is typically 180-250 ms, with packet loss at peak hours; payment requires an international credit card; and some platforms ban proxy use. If you need fast access from China, you'll have to pick a vendor that runs CN2 GIA optimized routes — that costs more, and mind the compliance side.

3. Cloudflare: The Patron Saint of Free Tiers

If the first two paths still cost money, Cloudflare has taken "freeloading" to its logical extreme — it stopped being just a CDN vendor long ago and is now a complete edge computing platform. For 90% of personal projects and small-to-medium applications, the free tier is enough.

Cloudflare's Deployment Philosophy: A Zero-Cost Layered Architecture

User request → Cloudflare CDN (edge node)
              ↓
    Pages (static frontend assets)
              ↓
    Workers (API / edge logic)
              ↓
      KV + R2 + D1 (the storage stack)
              ↓
    Tunnel → your own VPS (fallback for heavy jobs)
Take a real scenario: frontend on Pages, API on Workers, images in R2, data in D1, and heavy jobs routed back through Tunnel to your own $5 VPS — the whole stack's monthly cost can come down to the price of a single VPS. Next to the $88-320/month bill of a traditional "cloud server + database + CDN + object storage" setup, that advantage is an order of magnitude. Two traps to avoid: on the free Workers plan, CPU time is capped at 10 ms per request (time spent awaiting I/O doesn't count, so don't do CPU-heavy work there); and free-tier limits do get adjusted, so check the official pricing page before you launch. Paid plans start at Workers Paid $5/month, still a floor price.

5. A Verdict for Each Scenario

1. Personal blog / docs site / portfolio (zero budget first) → Cloudflare Pages + GitHub Pages. Connect a GitHub repo and deploys happen automatically: unlimited bandwidth plus a global CDN for not a single cent. Go with GitHub Pages for maximum simplicity, Cloudflare Pages for speed and extensibility. 2. A serious China-facing project / company website (filing required) → One of the big three domestic clouds. If budget is tight, read the renewal terms on the promotional pricing; if stability comes first, take Alibaba Cloud's 99 Plan or Tencent Cloud's new-and-existing-customer plans. Consider Huawei Cloud only if you need its ecosystem or domestic-stack (xinchuang) requirements. 3. Going global / sites aimed at overseas users → Start with Vultr (broad Asian coverage) or Hetzner (European price-performance), then move up to AWS once you've scaled. Don't forget to put Cloudflare CDN in front. 4. A personal project that needs a backend API plus a database → Two options: the full edge stack of Cloudflare Workers + D1 + R2 (cheapest), or a single overseas VPS where you install everything yourself (most flexible). If most of your users are in China, take a small instance from a domestic cloud. 5. You already have a server and want to cut costs and speed things up → No need to migrate. Just point your domain at Cloudflare's free plan: CDN acceleration, WAF protection, and free certificates in one go, plus origin IP masking.

How Is This Site Deployed?

Let this blog speak for itself — it runs exactly the "zero cost + low cost" hybrid approach:
  • Frontend: built with Vue 3 and pushed to GitHub Pages — free static hosting with version control built in.
  • Backend: a Django API deployed on PythonAnywhere's free tier, which is enough to serve the article endpoints and likes.
  • Images: served from Unsplash's remote image host, so local storage costs nothing.
It's a working example of running a complete frontend-plus-backend project without owning a server. If PythonAnywhere's limits ever fall short, the next step is to swap the backend for Cloudflare Workers and the images for R2 — and that migration path already exists. Which is exactly the point this article wants to make: a deployment plan isn't a one-time purchase. Start with a free or low-cost option, then evolve on demand as the business grows — that's the most pragmatic way to go to the cloud.

What Are the Pitfalls When Choosing a Deployment?

  1. Read the renewal terms carefully — a cheap first purchase with a doubled renewal is the biggest trap in domestic cloud; look for "same price on renewal".
  2. Egress is a hidden assassin — AWS and peers bill per GB, so run the numbers before you commit if you serve lots of images or downloads; R2 and Hetzner are free forever.
  3. Remember to cancel free trials — automatic billing at expiry is a classic incident; set billing alerts and budget caps.
  4. Always back data up off-site — cheap VPS plans make no redundancy promises; regular rsync/restic backups are the baseline.
  5. Compliance comes first — mainland China nodes require an ICP filing, and proxy-type uses need to respect the provider's TOS and local law.
